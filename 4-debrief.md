# 4. Debrief

A good debrief is short, structured, and ends with a decision.

**Length:** 20–30 minutes · **When:** within 2 business days of the final interview · **Who:** Everyone who interviewed, plus the decision-maker

## Before the meeting
- [ ] All scorecards submitted
- [ ] Recruiter shares a summary of the feedback (the [debrief-assistant](https://github.com/CandaceWalker/debrief-assistant) can help)
- [ ] Gaps flagged: any must-have nobody assessed

## Agenda

**1. Silent recap (2 min)**
Everyone rereads the summary. No discussion yet.

**2. Must-haves, one at a time (10 min)**
For each must-have: what's the evidence? Start with the interviewer who assessed it most directly.

**3. Disagreements (10 min)**
Where interviewers saw things differently, ask:
- Were we assessing the same thing?
- What specifically did each of us see?
- Does this gap matter for this role, or is it trainable?

**4. Decision (5 min)**
The decision-maker states the decision and the reasoning. Possible outcomes:
- **Hire:** move to offer
- **No hire:** recruiter closes the loop with the candidate
- **More information needed:** name exactly what's missing and how to get it (a follow-up conversation, a reference check)

## Facilitator tips
- **Have the most junior interviewers speak first,** so senior voices don't anchor everyone else.
- **Redirect vague feedback.** "Not a fit" → "What did you see that suggested that?"
- **Stop off-topic comments.** Anything about the candidate's personal life, appearance, or background unrelated to the work doesn't belong in the decision.
- **Compare to the bar, not to other candidates.**

## After the meeting
- Recruiter updates the candidate within 2 business days.
- Note anything to improve in the loop (for example, a must-have that was hard to assess).
